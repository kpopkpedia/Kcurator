Released in 1993 for the KBS drama **Lover (연인)**, Jeong Seok-won's soundtrack was far more than a collection of television themes.

Built around multiple versions of its title song alongside original instrumentals and a ballad sung by Kim Don-kyu, the album reflects the producer-driven studio experimentation that characterized Jeong's work in the early 1990s.

Best known as a co-founder and producer of **015B**, Jeong brought a distinctly pop-oriented approach to the project. Synthesizers, programmed rhythms, atmospheric arrangements, and dramatically different mixes of the same composition give *Lover* the character of a standalone studio album rather than a conventional collection of background music.

Today, the drama itself is less widely remembered than many Korean television hits that followed. Its soundtrack, however, remains an intriguing artifact of early-1990s Korean pop production.

# About the Drama

KBS2's *Lover (연인)* aired from May 1 to October 17, 1993, running for 50 episodes.

The series starred Shin Ae-ra, Lee Hyo-jung, Lee Hwi-hyang, Kim Joo-seung, and Jeon Hye-jin, among others.

While the drama belongs firmly to the television landscape of early-1990s Korea, its accompanying album is particularly interesting from a musical perspective. Instead of relying on one principal theme surrounded by incidental cues, the release was structured around multiple interpretations of its central song, original instrumental compositions, and a separate vocal ballad.

That structure gives *Lover* an identity that extends beyond the television series for which it was created.

---

# Jeong Seok-won's Producer-Driven Approach

By 1993, **Jeong Seok-won (정석원)** had already established himself as the creative force behind **015B**, one of the most distinctive project groups in early-1990s Korean pop.

015B was unusual in that it operated largely as a producer-centered project rather than around a permanent lead singer. Different vocalists could be brought in for different songs while Jeong Seok-won's composition and production provided musical continuity.

That philosophy is clearly visible on *Lover*.

Instead of building the album around a single singer, Jeong used different vocalists and arrangements to explore the same musical material from several directions. The title song alone appears four times, but each version approaches the composition differently.

The result feels less like repetition than a producer examining how far one melody can be reshaped through arrangement, rhythm, and vocal character.

---

# Jang Ho-il and the Original "Lover"

The album opens with **"Lover (Original Version)"**, sung by **Jang Ho-il (장호일)**, guitarist and co-founder of 015B.

Rather than turning the drama's main theme into a large-scale power ballad, the recording favors a comparatively restrained performance. Jang's vocal sits inside the arrangement rather than dominating it, leaving room for the electronic textures and rhythmic details surrounding the melody.

His voice also appears on the **Jamaican Mix** and **Vintage Mix**, allowing the same singer and composition to take on different characters through changes in production.

That makes the various versions of "Lover" more than simple bonus tracks. Together, they form the musical center of the album.

---

# Kim Don-kyu: "I'll Never Leave You Again"

Another important vocal presence is **Kim Don-kyu (김돈규)**.

He performs **"I'll Never Leave You Again" (다시는 너를 떠나지 않을 꺼야)**, written and composed by Jeong Seok-won.

Where the different versions of "Lover" emphasize arrangement and studio experimentation, Kim's song provides a more direct emotional counterpoint. His vocal style brings the album closer to the Korean pop-ballad tradition that would become increasingly prominent during the decade.

Placed among the remixes and instrumental tracks, the song broadens the soundtrack's musical palette without disrupting its overall atmosphere.

---

# Four Ways to Hear "Lover"

Perhaps the album's most distinctive idea is its repeated reinterpretation of the title song.

Rather than including one definitive version, the soundtrack contains four:

**Original Version**  
Sung by Jang Ho-il, this establishes the central melody and serves as the album's primary version of the theme.

**Jamaican Mix**  
Also featuring Jang Ho-il, this version reshapes the song through a more rhythm-centered arrangement influenced by Jamaican and reggae-associated musical ideas.

**Vintage Mix**  
Again sung by Jang Ho-il, the Vintage Mix offers another alternative treatment of the same composition.

**Soul II Soul Mix**  
This version features **Jo Sung-min (조성민)** rather than Jang Ho-il, giving the song both a different arrangement and a different vocal identity.

The naming of the Soul II Soul Mix also places the album unmistakably within the musical vocabulary of its time, when Korean producers were actively absorbing contemporary international dance, R&B, and electronic production trends.

Rather than treating these versions as incidental extras, *Lover* makes them part of the album's basic structure.

---

# The Instrumental Side of the Album

The soundtrack also contains three original instrumental pieces composed by Jeong Seok-won:

- **Morning (아침)**
- **And So We (그래서 우리는)**
- **Miro (미로)**

These tracks are important to the album's overall character.

They create breathing room between the vocal recordings and reinforce the sense that *Lover* was assembled as a complete listening sequence. The result moves between vocal pop, alternative mixes, ballad writing, and instrumental soundtrack music rather than simply repeating variations of the drama's main song.
