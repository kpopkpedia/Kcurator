When discussing the evolution of Korean popular music, few groups are as innovative—or as quietly influential—as **015B (공일오비)**.

Debuting in 1990, 015B challenged one of the most fundamental assumptions of pop music: that every group needed a permanent lead singer.

Instead, producers **Jeong Seok-won** and **Jang Ho-il** built the project around songwriting, arrangement, and studio production, inviting different vocalists to perform each song according to its unique style and emotion. At a time when most Korean acts were defined by charismatic frontmen, 015B demonstrated that producers themselves could become the creative identity of a group.

Their approach not only produced some of the most memorable songs of the 1990s but also introduced a new way of thinking about popular music—one that continues to influence Korean music production today.

# From Infinite Track to 015B

The story of 015B begins with **Infinite Track (무한궤도)**, the influential college band led by **Shin Hae-chul**. Their victory at the **1988 MBC Campus Song Festival** with **"To You" (그대에게)** made them one of the defining campus bands of late-1980s Korea.

Although Infinite Track quickly gained recognition, the band released only one studio album before disbanding in 1989.

For keyboardist **Jeong Seok-won** and bassist **Cho Hyung-gon**, the breakup became an opportunity rather than an ending. Together with guitarist **Jang Ho-il**, they began rethinking the very structure of a pop group.

The experience had revealed a weakness shared by many traditional bands. When a group depended heavily on one charismatic vocalist, its future often depended on that single individual. Creative disagreements or lineup changes could threaten the entire project.

Rather than replacing Shin Hae-chul with another frontman, the members asked a different question:

"What if the music came first, and the singer came second?"

The answer became 015B.

Instead of maintaining a permanent vocalist, the group selected singers whose voices best suited each composition. Songwriting, arranging, and production became the true center of the project, while the vocalists changed from song to song.

This producer-led philosophy became 015B's defining characteristic and set the group apart from virtually every other Korean act of its era.

Interestingly, the transition was never intended as a rejection of Infinite Track. Shin Hae-chul himself appeared as a guest vocalist on 015B's debut album, symbolizing the continued friendship and artistic respect shared among the former bandmates.

# Why the Name "015B"?

The name **015B**, pronounced *Gong-Il-O-Bi*, has fascinated fans for decades.

According to guitarist **Jang Ho-il**, the name originated from founding member **Cho Hyung-gon's** user ID on **KETEL**, one of Korea's earliest PC communication networks. His ID, **01orbit**, was eventually stylized into the distinctive name **015B**, giving the project a modern and unconventional identity.

Over the years, however, fans developed another interpretation.

Because the founding members came from **Infinite Track (무한궤도)**, many believed the name served as a numerical representation of their former band. Although Jang Ho-il later clarified that this explanation was not the group's original intention, the story remains one of the best-known pieces of trivia surrounding 015B.

The group also embraced a poetic Hanja interpretation of its name:

空一烏飛 (Gong Il O Bi)

meaning "A lone crow flying across an empty sky."

While unrelated to the official origin of the name, the phrase beautifully reflects the group's independent and unconventional artistic identity.

# The Guest Vocalist System

🎥 015B – "The Love of a New Generation" (SBS New Generation Assembly, 1994)
{% include youtube.html url="[https://www.youtube.com/embed/gS2IkVZ6yrA](https://www.youtube.com/embed/gS2IkVZ6yrA)" %}

Before 015B, Korean pop groups were almost always built around a permanent lead singer. 015B turned that convention upside down.

Jeong Seok-won and Jang Ho-il handled composing, arranging, producing, and much of the instrumental performance themselves, while inviting different singers to perform individual tracks. Every song was matched with the vocalist whose voice best expressed its mood rather than forcing every composition to fit the same performer.

This approach also allowed listeners to experience an unusually wide range of vocal colors within a single album—something rarely seen in Korean popular music at the time.

The system introduced audiences to numerous talented singers, including **Yoon Jong-shin**, **Kim Don-kyoo**, **Lee Jang-woo**, and **Jo Sung-min**, many of whom later established successful solo careers.

Although producer-led collaborations became increasingly common in later decades, 015B demonstrated the artistic potential of this model remarkably early in Korean pop history.

# A Sophisticated Musical Style

🎥 015B - "Very Old Lovers" (KBS Gayo Top 10, 1993)
{% include youtube.html url="[https://www.youtube.com/embed/62w3HOwwaIc](https://www.youtube.com/embed/62w3HOwwaIc)" %}

While much of Korean popular music in the early 1990s emphasized dance music or traditional ballads, 015B cultivated a distinctly urban and studio-oriented sound.

Drawing inspiration from contemporary Western pop, jazz, funk, soft rock, and city pop while maintaining a uniquely Korean sensibility, the group created music that felt sophisticated without sacrificing accessibility.

Their recordings are characterized by elegant harmonies, rich keyboard textures, tasteful guitar work, and meticulous arrangements. Rather than relying on spectacle or choreography, 015B focused on musical craftsmanship, creating songs that continue to sound remarkably fresh decades later.

# Legacy

More than three decades after its debut, 015B remains one of the defining names in Korean popular music.

Its greatest contribution extends beyond its catalog of hit songs. By placing producers at the center of the creative process and embracing a rotating cast of guest vocalists, 015B expanded the possibilities of what a Korean pop group could be.

Their emphasis on songwriting, arrangement, and studio craftsmanship helped broaden the musical landscape of the 1990s and demonstrated that artistic identity could be built around creative vision rather than a single performer.

Today, 015B continues to be admired not only as one of Korea's most distinctive project groups but also as a symbol of musical innovation and enduring quality.

### Curator's Note

Among the many artists featured in **K-pedia**, 015B occupies a unique place.

What makes the group remarkable is not simply its nostalgia or its impressive catalog of songs, but how surprisingly contemporary its music still feels. Every revisit reveals another layer of Jeong Seok-won's elegant arrangements, Jang Ho-il's understated guitar work, and the meticulous production that defines every recording.

Listening to 015B today feels less like revisiting the early 1990s than discovering timeless pop craftsmanship. Long before producer-led collaborations became commonplace, they quietly demonstrated that enduring popular music begins with exceptional songwriting, thoughtful arrangement, and an uncompromising commitment to musical excellence.
