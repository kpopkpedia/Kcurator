## Who Was Shin Hae-chul?

**Shin Hae-chul (신해철, 1968–2014)** was a singer, songwriter, producer, and radio host whose career moved through almost every corner of Korean popular music. He began as a campus-festival winner, became a teen idol, led one of Korea's most ambitious rock bands, experimented with electronic music, and spent his later years as a late-night radio host and one of the country's most outspoken cultural commentators.

What connects all of these roles is a single idea: that popular music could ask serious questions. He wrote, arranged, and produced his own work at a time when that was rare in Korean pop, and he treated his listeners, especially young ones, as people capable of thinking for themselves. To many Koreans he is still simply **"Mawang" (마왕, the Demon King)**.

This series follows his career in three parts. This first part is an overview and guide. Parts 2 and 3 tell the full story.

## At a Glance

| Year | Milestone |
| ---- | --------- |
| 1988 | Wins the Grand Prize at the MBC Campus Song Festival with Infinite Track (무한궤도) and "To You" (그대에게) |
| 1990 | Begins his solo career |
| 1991 | Releases *Myself*, featuring "Jazz Café" (재즈 카페) |
| 1992 | Forms the rock band **N.EX.T** and releases *Home* |
| 1996 | Forms the electronic duo **No Dance** (노댄스) with Yoon Sang |
| 1997 | Releases the *Lazenca* space rock opera project with N.EX.T |
| 2000s | Hosts the late-night radio program *Ghost Station* (고스트 스테이션) |
| 2014 | Passes away at the age of 46 |

## Where It All Began

🎥 Infinite Track — "To You" (그대에게)
{% include youtube.html url="https://www.youtube.com/embed/SVxiqGiLMCM" %}

*Infinite Track's debut song, "To You" (그대에게).*

More than three decades after it won the 1988 Campus Song Festival, "To You" is still played at university festivals, sporting events, and graduation ceremonies across Korea. For many listeners it is the first Shin Hae-chul song they ever heard, and it remains the best place to start.

### Curator's Note

Shin Hae-chul is difficult to summarize because he never stayed in one place long enough. Each time he found success, he moved on, from campus anthems to synth-pop, from synth-pop to rock opera, from the stage to the radio booth.

That restlessness is exactly why he matters. His career shows how much Korean popular music changed between the late 1980s and the 2010s, and how one artist could push it forward at almost every stage.
